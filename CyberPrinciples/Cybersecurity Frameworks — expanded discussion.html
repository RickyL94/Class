CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORKS / SIMPLE GUIDE

Seven kinds of security guidance. Seven different jobs.

First see the whole map. Then ask of each category: what do we have, and what is it for?

One continuing example: a retailer stores customer data, takes card payments and faces ransomware.

01 / LAW & REGULATION

What must we protect by law?

What we have
EU GDPRRules for personal data within its scope.
UK GDPR + DPA 2018UK data-protection duties.
HIPAAUS health-information rules for covered entities and business associates.
SOXUS financial-reporting and internal-control duties.
What are they for?

They set duties for the organisations and information they cover.

Cybersecurity helps meet those duties by protecting data, restricting access, keeping records and responding to incidents.
Not every law applies to every organisation.
CYBER LINK If customer data is stolen, the retailer checks its UK data-protection duties. HIPAA and SOX are comparison examples, not automatic retailer duties.
Scope matters before any compliance claim.ICO · HHS · SEC
01 / LAW & REGULATION / WORKED EXAMPLE

A customer-data breach: what must we decide?

An attacker uses a stolen staff login to export the retailer’s customer-address file.

FIND

Which personal data left the system and whose was it?

DECIDE

Assess the likely risk to people; check whether UK GDPR notification duties apply.

PROVE

Keep account logs, the data inventory and a dated breach assessment.

THE POINT Security response and legal assessment happen together. An incident is not automatically reportable.

02 / INDUSTRY STANDARDS & SCHEMES

What does our work ask us to meet?

What we have
PCI DSS v4.0.1Security requirements for payment-card account data.
Cyber EssentialsUK government-backed baseline certification around five technical controls.
Cyber Essentials is a certification scheme, not a payment-industry standard.
What are they for?

They turn a context into practical security expectations.

PCI DSS focuses on payment-card security. Cyber Essentials helps organisations demonstrate basic protection against common attacks.
CYBER LINK The retailer maps where card data flows, then checks access, system configuration, updates and malware protection.
Exact requirements depend on scope and the scheme’s current version.PCI SSC · NCSC
02 / INDUSTRY STANDARDS & SCHEMES / WORKED EXAMPLE

A hosted checkout does not end the security conversation

The retailer uses a payment provider for checkout and has several laptops with overdue updates.

SCOPE

Map the checkout flow and the provider’s responsibilities.

APPLY

Check PCI DSS duties for payment data and Cyber Essentials for baseline device hygiene.

PROVE

Keep the provider agreement, compliance evidence and laptop-update reports.

THE POINT A provider can reduce scope, but the retailer must still understand and validate its responsibilities.

03 / MANAGEMENT STANDARDS

How do we run security consistently?

What we have
ISO/IEC 27000Explains the information-security management-system family and its concepts.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022Requirements for an information security management system (ISMS).
What are they for?

To manage security risks as a repeatable organisational process.

Decide what matters, assign owners, choose protections, check them and improve. It is more than buying a security product.
CYBER LINK If the retailer deploys MFA, its ISMS asks who owns it, which accounts are covered and how exceptions are reviewed.
ISMS = information security management systemISO 27000 · ISO 27001
03 / MANAGEMENT STANDARDS / WORKED EXAMPLE

Security cannot depend on one person remembering

The retailer introduced MFA after a breach, but some administrator accounts remain exempt.

DECIDE

Identify the risk and assign an owner for administrator access.

DO

Roll out MFA and document justified exceptions.

CHECK

Review coverage, test exceptions and improve when gaps appear.

THE POINT ISO/IEC 27001 is about a managed security system, not a claim that buying MFA is enough.

04 / RISK FRAMEWORKS

How do we organise cyber risk?

What we have
NIST CSF 2.0A framework for describing and managing cybersecurity outcomes.
Six connected functions: Govern · Identify · Protect · Detect · Respond · Recover.
What is it for?

To see the whole risk picture, not just the firewall.

It helps leaders and technical teams decide what to prioritise, who is responsible and whether they can detect and recover from attacks.
CYBER LINK For ransomware: know the affected systems, limit spread, spot the incident, respond and restore service.
The functions are concurrent, not a strict step-by-step sequence.SOURCE · NIST
04 / RISK FRAMEWORKS / WORKED EXAMPLE

Use NIST CSF to see the whole incident

Ransomware stops the retailer’s online ordering. Backups exist, but nobody knows who leads the response.

BEFORE

Govern: name the owner. Identify critical systems. Protect access and data.

DURING

Detect unusual activity. Respond by containing affected systems.

AFTER

Recover from tested backups and improve the plan.

THE POINT The six functions are connected outcomes, not six boxes to tick in order.

05 / CONTROL GUIDANCE

Which safeguards should we choose?

What we have
ISO/IEC 27002Guidance on information-security controls and their implementation.
NIST SP 800-53A detailed catalogue of security and privacy controls.
CIS Controls v8.1Prioritised, practical safeguards; IG1 is the starting group.
What are they for?

To move from a risk to a suitable protection.

Use guidance to select, adapt and check controls. A catalogue does not mean every listed control applies to every organisation.
CYBER LINK If unmanaged laptops are the risk, start by finding the devices, securing their setup and checking update coverage.
IG = Implementation GroupISO · NIST · CIS

06 / THREAT KNOWLEDGE

How does the attacker behave?

What we have
MITRE ATT&CKA knowledge base of observed attacker tactics and techniques.
Tactic = the attacker’s goal. Technique = how they try to achieve it.
What is it for?

To turn an attack story into testable defensive questions.

Teams use it to ask whether their logs, alerts and safeguards cover behaviour attackers actually use. It is not a compliance certificate.
CYBER LINK A stolen account is used to explore other systems. Which logs would show credential misuse and movement between machines?
Use evidence to map behaviour; do not guess a technique from a headline.SOURCE · MITRE
06 / THREAT KNOWLEDGE / WORKED EXAMPLE

Describe the attacker with evidence, not guesses

A compromised account signs in and then accesses a second machine it does not normally use.

OBSERVE

Authentication logs show an unusual login and remote access.

INTERPRET

Investigate credential misuse and possible lateral movement in ATT&CK.

TEST

Check whether alerts would have detected both behaviours.

THE POINT ATT&CK describes observed adversary behaviour; it does not prove an exact technique without evidence.

07 / SECURITY CONTROLS

What do we actually put in place?

What we have · by job
PreventiveStop or reduce an attack: MFA.
DetectiveNotice suspicious activity: sign-in alert.
CorrectiveLimit damage or restore: tested backup.
What we have · by type
PhysicalLocks and badges.
ProceduralPolicies and response plans.
TechnicalMFA, logging and encryption.
WHAT ARE THEY FOR? To prevent, notice and recover from attacks. Job and type classify the same safeguard.
CYBER LINK Against a stolen password: MFA may prevent misuse, alerts may detect it and a response plan helps contain damage.
These are the working defences—the other categories help choose and govern them.
07 / SECURITY CONTROLS / WORKED EXAMPLE

Build layers that stop, spot and repair

A stolen password is used to try to reach the retailer’s order system.

PREVENT

MFA can block the login: technical, preventive.

DETECT

A sign-in alert can flag the attempt: technical, detective.

CORRECT

Disable the account and restore affected systems: response procedure plus tested backup.

THE POINT Classify by job and by type. A single control can contribute to more than one security outcome.
