Project Brief

INTRODUCTION

Your goal: automate one worthwhile, repetitive business task. Choose your own scenario; explain why it matters and why a custom script is appropriate. A well-justified original or challenging case may earn higher marks.

Build one working solution and submit it in two connected parts:

  • Task 1 — Portfolio (70%): a PDF of up to 3,500 words, source scripts, configuration/data files, and a remote Git repository link.
  • Task 2 — Presentation (30%): a 7-minute briefing and demonstration, with slides in PDF or PowerPoint.

Tasks, Evidence & Submission

Task 1: Automation Solution Portfolio (70%)

Report deadline: 18 December 2026 · Maximum 3,500 words.

Suggested report structure

Use these four sections to explain your work. Refer to your submitted files and evidence; do not paste complete scripts or the README into the report (Screenshots are fine).

1. Process analysis and requirementsLO1

Explain the problem and what your solution needs to achieve.

  • Introduce the task, users and problems with the current process.
  • Include your As-Is and To-Be maps.
  • Summarise inputs, outputs, requirements, assumptions and constraints.
  • State your acceptance criteria.

2. Solution design and implementationLO2

Explain how your Python solution works.

  • Describe its main functions and processing steps.
  • Explain validation, error handling and logging.
  • Use selected code extracts to support your explanation.
  • Describe a fault you corrected and how you checked the correction.

3. Version control and documentationLO3

Explain how your work is organised and can be used by someone else.

  • Provide your repository link and selected commit evidence.
  • Show the project folder structure.
  • Reference your README and briefly explain how it supports setup, operation and troubleshooting.

4. Testing and evaluationLO3

Explain how you checked the solution and what you learned.

  • Present a short test plan covering valid input, invalid input and an edge case, linked to the acceptance criteria.
  • Summarise the results of two or three simple pytest tests, including correct processing and validation or error handling, and reference the evidence.
  • Discuss security, limitations and any untested criteria.
  • Evaluate the outcome against the original problem and identify improvements.

What you must submit — Task 1

Submit through Canvas as instructed in the final submission area:

  • Portfolio report PDF, maximum 3,500 words
  • Complete Python source/script files
  • Configuration and sample data files, with secrets removed
  • Pytest test file and results evidence
  • README.md and relevant supporting outputs, logs and repository evidence
  • Accessible remote Git repository link

Task 2: Professional Demonstration & Briefing (30%)

Live presentation: 11 December 2026 · 7 minutes. Pitch the completed solution to organisational stakeholders:

  1. Explain the problem and why scripting beats suitable off-the-shelf or low-code alternatives.
  2. Demonstrate input → script → output, plus an intentional bad input handled safely.
  3. Show Git history and selected pytest results. Explain which valid, invalid and edge-case scenarios were tested and which remain planned.
  4. Discuss limitations, security, business value and the non-technical user handoff.

Task 2 submission check

  • Slide deck (.pdf or .pptx)
  • Working live demonstration ready to show

Before you submit

Confirm with the tutor: the report submission time on 18 December 2026, your live presentation slot on 11 December 2026, the Canvas link, marking rubric and any slide-upload arrangements.

Academic integrity: submit your own work and follow the institution’s rules on referencing and AI use. For extensions and late submissions, rely on the current official regulations and Canvas details rather than the unfinished alternatives in the source template.

Assessment and Tutor Feedback / Academic Regulations

Physical resources

The brief specifies a laptop with minimum spec.

Grades and feedback

Grades and feedback will be released within, or up to a maximum of, 20 working days after the assessment submission date.

Academic misconduct

Under the General Academic Regulations, plagiarism, inappropriate use of AI applications and tools, collusion, fabrication of information, contract cheating and other academic misconduct are handled formally.

Examples that may lead to an investigation include close similarity between submissions, suspected misuse of AI, third-party completion, and work that is not appropriately referenced. The brief states that the penalty is a fail grade with an additional penalty according to the gravity of the case.

If in doubt about plagiarism, AI use, or how group work recognises individual contributions, discuss it with the module tutor or Course Leader. At submission, students must complete or sign a declaration that the work is their own.

Late submissions — source template has two alternatives

Option A (ordinary graded assessment): work may be submitted up to 24 hours late, capped at a pass grade unless a five-day approved extension or other approved Extenuating Circumstances (ECs) applies.

Option B (Pass/Fail assessment): no 24-hour late allowance, but students may still apply for a five-day extension or ECs.
